## P1: Diagonal anchor screw heads are embedded in the brace

In `model.py:rev3_hardware`, every rear screw starts at the crossbar's recessed bearing plane, Y=206. The diagonal branch adds its 6 mm thickness, yielding Y=212.

The diagonal occupies Y=210..216 and has plain 4.5 mm clearance holes, with no head counterbores. Its screw head must bear on Y=216. The current head occupies Y=212..216, inside the brace material.

Independent Build123d intersections against each individual diagonal solid:

| Anchor X/Z | Current Y=212 screw / matching brace | Correct bearing plane Y=216 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 35 / 55 | 163.3628 mm3 | 0 mm3 |
| 246 / 335 | 163.3628 mm3 | 0 mm3 |

These values use freshly constructed screw geometry for each placement and intersections with individual brace solids. Aggregate compound Boolean results were inconsistent and were not used for the finding.

Correction: derive each screw's start from its own actual bearing face. For diagonal anchors use `BODY_DEPTH_MM + REAR_CROSSBAR_THICK_MM + REAR_BRACE_THICK_MM`, without subtracting the rear crossbar counterbore depth. At Y=216, the 16 mm screw traverses 6 mm of brace and 10 mm into the crossbar. Its 11 mm pilot passes through the 10 mm crossbar, so the nominal extra millimeter is open space behind the bar, not a blind bottom within it.

`test_rev3_hardware_paths_clear_printed_parts` includes rails, columns and crossbars but omits the diagonal solids. Add both halves to the hardware collision check and verify bearing-plane contact. The current 65 passing tests therefore do not catch this regression.
